# Build a standalone, accurate prototype first, and integrate with PickleCam afterwards

On 20 October 2026 PickleCam's backend developer told us the priority is a working prototype, not a pipeline that collects videos as soon as they reach Bunny, and not cost optimization. PickleCam is already successful and will only pay for stats and reels that players trust, so DinkData is a standalone app until PickleCam approves the prototype. M0 to M3 build a prototype accurate and reliable enough to show PickleCam's CEO at the end of M3, measured by the accuracy gates on held-out facilities.

The prototype works on about 150 games downloaded by hand from the facility dashboard. `dink ingest` prepares each one and records its camera, and `dink run <video_id>` writes the report and a reel MP4 per player to a folder. Each new camera gets a one-time hand calibration. The prototype processes every frame, and eval reports GPU time as information. R2 stays the shared development store, and development keeps its $50 monthly budget, which a training run that measurably improves accuracy can exceed if both owners agree.

M4 becomes Integration and beta and starts after PickleCam approves the prototype. It holds the read-only endpoint, `dink catalog sync`, automatic sampling, the daily fetch, the pull zone and bandwidth, the cost target and provider benchmark, the job queue and results-only storage, the batch runner, the web player over PickleCam's video, loose-file input, automatic camera matching and calibration, delivery to players and the beta with review. We build those around whatever PickleCam already runs, and we keep the contracts, timestamps and HMAC ids that make the switch cheap.
